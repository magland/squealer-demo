# squealer-demo

A browser implementation of the Squealer from Gelman, Jaffe, Carlson and Greengard, "The Squealer: sensification of model exploration and model misfit" (arXiv:2606.29842, 2026).

Live: https://magland.github.io/squealer-demo/ (deployed from `main` by GitHub Actions).

Starting from the best-fit curve g(x | θ̂), the user grabs the curve at any point and drags it. The parameters follow as the most probable values for which the curve passes through the held point: θ* maximizes log p(θ | y) subject to g(x_j | θ*) = y_j. This is the limit σ* → 0 of the paper's pseudo-data method, without the lag of a soft pull and without a σ* to choose. On release the point stays as a pin, so the curve stays put and later grabs build on it; pins can be dragged or released (right-click). At most as many pins as parameters hold at once; a further grab releases the oldest.

The constrained mode is found by an augmented Lagrangian with warm-started Nelder-Mead inner steps (about 5 ms per drag step for golf). Constraints are scaled by the posterior sd of g(x_j) from a Laplace approximation at θ̂. If no θ passes through all pins, the curve gets as close as it can and the app reports the miss.

The drop in log posterior, log p(θ̂ | y) − log p(θ* | y), drives a squeal that rises in pitch, loudness and roughness, and each data point is colored by its change in log likelihood (red worse, green better).

As in the paper's Figure 1, two panels sit below the main plot. The dashboard has one row per parameter, then log prior, log likelihood and log posterior; each row shows the posterior density of that quantity, θ̂ (blue) and θ* (red), centered at θ̂ and labeled with the shift of θ* in posterior sd. The second panel plots log p(y_i | x_i, θ*) − log p(y_i | x_i, θ̂) against x_i. The main plot also shows light curves g(x | θ^s) for posterior draws. The draws come from a normal (Laplace) approximation at θ̂ rather than MCMC; for golf the posterior is so concentrated that the approximation is accurate and the draw curves are indistinguishable from the best fit, as the paper notes.

## Examples

- Golf putting (Broadie 2018; Gelman 2019): 31 distance bins, about 1.25 million putts, two parameters. Data and model as in the authors' Python prototype, https://github.com/elc45/squealer-prototype. Posterior mode (0.01329, 0.13725) matches the prototype.
- Serial dilution assay (paper Section 2.2, after Gelman, Chew and Shnaidman 2004). The paper does not state its model or publish its plate, so model and data are our reconstruction of the structure it describes (data from `scripts/gen_dilution.py`, seeded). One standard of known concentration at 14 two-fold dilutions and 23 unknown samples at three dilutions each; log y ~ normal(log g_j(d), σ_j) with g_j(d) = β1 + β2 / (1 + (c_j d / β3)^(−β4)) shared across samples, log c_j ~ normal(μ_c, τ_c) (the paper's hierarchical per-sample β2 term) and log σ_j ~ normal(μ_σ, 1). Samples 3 and 23 do not follow the curve shape, as in the paper's Figure 7. The model has 54 parameters, so the mode and the constrained fits use L-BFGS with analytic gradients (about 11 ms per drag step). The display has one small panel per sample (Figure 7, with log dilution on the x axis) and a per-sample scatter of log c against log σ with ±1 posterior sd (Figure 9). θ̂ is the joint posterior mode. With an estimated group scale for the σ_j, that mode collapses (all σ_j equal, τ_σ → 0; the problem noted in the paper's Section 1.4); a non-centered parameterization avoids the collapse but overstates τ_c (4.5 against a simulated 1.0), so we fix the σ group scale at 1 instead. Pinning samples 3 and 23 up to their 1:100 readings costs 2.5 in log posterior, all of it in the prior, and τ_c rises from 1.46 to 1.63, which is the paper's finding.
- Body temperature (paper Section 2.4): y ~ normal(f(t), σ), f ~ GP(98.6, k) with squared exponential kernel, hyperparameters (α, ℓ, σ) fit by maximizing the log marginal likelihood, time scaled to [0, 1] over 6:00 to 12:00. The paper's data are not published, so we simulate 81 readings shaped like its Figure 11a (generated by `scripts/gen_temperature.py`, seeded). A grab conditions f(x*) = y* exactly, the limit of the paper's pseudo-data point with variance 1e-6 (its eq. 10); α, ℓ, σ are re-fit on the readings and pins together, and the curve is the posterior mean of f, which passes through every pin. The cost is the drop in log marginal likelihood of the readings plus ½ dᵀV⁻¹d, the squared distance of the pins from the data-only posterior of f in posterior sd. Light curves are draws of f given the readings at θ̂. As in the paper's Table 1, pulling the curve up toward the late high readings mostly raises σ (0.091 to 0.148 with one pin at 11:00, 100.3 °F; the paper reports 0.10 to 0.14).

## Layout

- `src/examples/`: the `Example` interface the UI uses (`types.ts`), an adapter for parametric models (`parametric.ts`), the GP example (`gpTemperature.ts`), the dilution assay (`dilution.ts`).
- `src/gradSquealer.ts`: mode, Laplace covariance and constrained fit for models with analytic gradients (L-BFGS).
- `src/models/`: parametric models implementing `CurveModel` (`types.ts`); `golf.ts`.
- `src/squealer.ts`: base fit, Laplace curve sd, constrained fit.
- `src/stats/`: erfc with accurate tails, Nelder-Mead, L-BFGS, Laplace covariance, Cholesky, seeded normal draws.
- `src/squeal.ts`: Web Audio squeal.
- `src/Plot.tsx`, `src/Dashboard.tsx`, `src/PointChange.tsx`, `src/GroupScatter.tsx`, `src/App.tsx`: UI.
